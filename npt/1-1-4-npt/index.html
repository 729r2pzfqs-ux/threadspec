1-1/4-11.5 NPT Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

1-1/4 NPT, written in full as 1-1/4-11.5 NPT, is the American taper pipe thread for nominal 1-1/4 inch pipe: 11.5 threads per inch cut on a 1 in 16 taper, on pipe with an outside diameter of 1.660 in (42.16 mm). The nominal size names the pipe and is not a dimension you can measure on the thread.

Where the diameters are measured

A taper thread has no single pitch diameter. ASME B1.20.1 defines it at the end of the pipe (E0), at the plane where a fitting stops when screwed on by hand (E1), and at the end of the effective thread (E2). Each step of 1 in along the axis adds 1/16 in to the diameter.

1-1/4 NPT tap drill size

Tap drill for 1-1/4 NPT: 1-1/2" (1.5000 in, 38.10 mm)

This is the size printed on standard tap drill charts. ASME B1.20.1 suggests the slightly smaller 1-31/64" (1.4844 in) for a fuller thread.

The hole for a taper tap is a compromise, because a straight drilled hole meets a tapered thread. The minor diameter of the thread is 1.4876 in at the small end (K0) and grows by 1/16 in per inch of depth. The chart size of 1-1/2" is 0.0156 in larger than the 1-31/64" the standard suggests: it taps more easily but leaves shallower threads at the bottom of the hole. Use the smaller drill where the joint has to hold high pressure or the material is soft. For the fullest thread, drill with the reaming size and follow with a 1 in 16 taper pipe reamer.

ASME B1.20.1 notes that its suggested drills do not guarantee fully formed threads over the whole hand-tight length.

How far 1-1/4 NPT screws in

A 1-1/4 NPT fitting turns in by hand for 0.420 in (10.7 mm), about 4.8 threads, before the flanks meet. The standard then allows 3 more threads of wrench make-up, for a total engagement of about 0.681 in (17.3 mm). Tolerance on the hand-tight position is one thread either way, so a joint that stops a turn early or late is still within the standard.

NPT threads do not seal by themselves. The crests and roots leave a spiral gap, so ASME B1.20.1 calls for a sealant (PTFE tape or pipe compound) where the joint must be pressure-tight. Dryseal NPTF threads to ASME B1.20.3 have the same pitch and taper but controlled crests and roots that seal without it.

1-1/4 NPT against G1-1/4 BSP

The British Standard Pipe thread of the same nominal size has a different pitch (11 against 11.5 threads per inch), a 55° flank angle instead of 60° and a major diameter of 41.910 mm against 42.164 mm. The two do not screw together properly. Use an adapter.
