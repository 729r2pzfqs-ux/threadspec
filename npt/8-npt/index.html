8-8 NPT Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

8 NPT, written in full as 8-8 NPT, is the American taper pipe thread for nominal 8 inch pipe: 8 threads per inch cut on a 1 in 16 taper, on pipe with an outside diameter of 8.625 in (219.07 mm). The nominal size names the pipe and is not a dimension you can measure on the thread.

Where the diameters are measured

A taper thread has no single pitch diameter. ASME B1.20.1 defines it at the end of the pipe (E0), at the plane where a fitting stops when screwed on by hand (E1), and at the end of the effective thread (E2). Each step of 1 in along the axis adds 1/16 in to the diameter.

8 NPT tap drill size

Bore before tapping 8 NPT: 8.3336 in (211.67 mm)

This is K0, the basic minor diameter at the small end of the thread.

No tap drill is published for 8 NPT. Threads this large are bored or milled, and ASME B1.20.1 gives the minor diameter K0 as the basis for the hole size. Taper the bore at 1 in 16 where full threads are needed over the whole engagement.

How far 8 NPT screws in

A 8 NPT fitting turns in by hand for 1.063 in (27.0 mm), about 8.5 threads, before the flanks meet. The standard then allows 2 more threads of wrench make-up, for a total engagement of about 1.313 in (33.4 mm). Tolerance on the hand-tight position is one thread either way, so a joint that stops a turn early or late is still within the standard.

NPT threads do not seal by themselves. The crests and roots leave a spiral gap, so ASME B1.20.1 calls for a sealant (PTFE tape or pipe compound) where the joint must be pressure-tight. Dryseal NPTF threads to ASME B1.20.3 have the same pitch and taper but controlled crests and roots that seal without it.
