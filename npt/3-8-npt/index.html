3/8-18 NPT Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

3/8 NPT, written in full as 3/8-18 NPT, is the American taper pipe thread for nominal 3/8 inch pipe: 18 threads per inch cut on a 1 in 16 taper, on pipe with an outside diameter of 0.675 in (17.14 mm). The nominal size names the pipe and is not a dimension you can measure on the thread.

3/8 NPT is used for air lines, fuel fittings and small hydraulic connections.

Where the diameters are measured

A taper thread has no single pitch diameter. ASME B1.20.1 defines it at the end of the pipe (E0), at the plane where a fitting stops when screwed on by hand (E1), and at the end of the effective thread (E2). Each step of 1 in along the axis adds 1/16 in to the diameter.

The hole for a taper tap is a compromise, because a straight drilled hole meets a tapered thread. The minor diameter of the thread is 0.5676 in at the small end (K0) and grows by 1/16 in per inch of depth. Many printed charts give 37/64" for 3/8 NPT. That is 0.0156 in larger than the drill the standard suggests: it taps more easily but leaves shallower threads at the bottom of the hole. For the fullest thread, drill with the smaller size and follow with a 1 in 16 taper pipe reamer.

ASME B1.20.1 notes that its suggested drills do not guarantee fully formed threads over the whole hand-tight length.

How far 3/8 NPT screws in

A 3/8 NPT fitting turns in by hand for 0.240 in (6.1 mm), about 4.3 threads, before the flanks meet. The standard then allows 3 more threads of wrench make-up, for a total engagement of about 0.407 in (10.3 mm). Tolerance on the hand-tight position is one thread either way, so a joint that stops a turn early or late is still within the standard.

NPT threads do not seal by themselves. The crests and roots leave a spiral gap, so ASME B1.20.1 calls for a sealant (PTFE tape or pipe compound) where the joint must be pressure-tight. Dryseal NPTF threads to ASME B1.20.3 have the same pitch and taper but controlled crests and roots that seal without it.

3/8 NPT against G3/8 BSP

The British Standard Pipe thread of the same nominal size has a different pitch (19 against 18 threads per inch), a 55° flank angle instead of 60° and a major diameter of 16.662 mm against 17.145 mm. The two do not screw together properly. Use an adapter.
