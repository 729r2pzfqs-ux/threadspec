M39×3 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M39×3 is an ISO metric fine-pitch thread: the 39 mm diameter with a 3 mm pitch (8.5 threads per inch) instead of the coarse 4 mm of M39×4. The thread is 0.54 mm shallower, so the tensile stress area rises from 976 to 1030 mm² (5% more) and the lead angle falls from 2.00° to 1.48°.

  • Pitch3 mm8.5 TPI

M39×3 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 48 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.048 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M39×3 tap drill size

Tap drill for M39×3: 36 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 39 − 3 = 36 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 35.752 and 36.252 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M39×3

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M39×3. The nearest is 1-1/2-12 UNF, which is 0.90 mm smaller in diameter with a pitch of 2.117 mm against 3.000 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M39×3 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 3 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 2.598 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 39 − 1.949 = 37.051 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 39 − 3.248 = 35.752 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 39 − 3.681 = 35.319 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 36.185² = 1028.39 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 1.48°
