M18×1.5 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M18×1.5 is an ISO metric fine-pitch thread: the 18 mm diameter with a 1.5 mm pitch (16.9 threads per inch) instead of the coarse 2.5 mm of M18×2.5. The thread is 0.54 mm shallower, so the tensile stress area rises from 192 to 216 mm² (12% more) and the lead angle falls from 2.78° to 1.61°.

M18×1.5 is used for oxygen (lambda) sensors and for larger spark plugs.

M18×1.5 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 32 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.032 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M18×1.5 tap drill size

Tap drill for M18×1.5: 16.5 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 18 − 1.5 = 16.5 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 16.376 and 16.676 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M18×1.5

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M18×1.5. The nearest is 3/4-16 UNF, which is 1.05 mm larger in diameter with a pitch of 1.587 mm against 1.500 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M18×1.5 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 1.5 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 1.299 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 18 − 0.974 = 17.026 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 18 − 1.624 = 16.376 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 18 − 1.840 = 16.160 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 16.593² = 216.23 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 1.61°
