M20×1.5 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M20×1.5 is an ISO metric fine-pitch thread: the 20 mm diameter with a 1.5 mm pitch (16.9 threads per inch) instead of the coarse 2.5 mm of M20×2.5. The thread is 0.54 mm shallower, so the tensile stress area rises from 245 to 272 mm² (11% more) and the lead angle falls from 2.48° to 1.44°.

M20×1.5 is the most widely used metric cable gland and conduit entry thread.

M20×1.5 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 32 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.032 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M20×1.5 tap drill size

Tap drill for M20×1.5: 18.5 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 20 − 1.5 = 18.5 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 18.376 and 18.676 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M20×1.5

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M20×1.5. The nearest is 3/4-16 UNF, which is 0.95 mm smaller in diameter with a pitch of 1.587 mm against 1.500 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M20×1.5 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 1.5 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 1.299 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 20 − 0.974 = 19.026 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 20 − 1.624 = 18.376 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 20 − 1.840 = 18.160 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 18.593² = 271.50 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 1.44°
