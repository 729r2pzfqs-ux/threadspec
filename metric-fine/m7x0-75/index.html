M7×0.75 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M7×0.75 is an ISO metric fine-pitch thread: the 7 mm diameter with a 0.75 mm pitch (33.9 threads per inch) instead of the coarse 1 mm of M7×1. The thread is 0.14 mm shallower, so the tensile stress area rises from 28.9 to 31.1 mm² (8% more) and the lead angle falls from 2.87° to 2.10°.

M7×0.75 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 22 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.022 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M7×0.75 tap drill size

Tap drill for M7×0.75: 6.2 mm

This leaves about 82% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 7 − 0.75 = 6.25 mm. Tap drill tables round this to 6.2 mm, a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 6.188 and 6.378 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M7×0.75

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

Clearance hole for M7

The clearance hole depends only on the 7 mm diameter, not on the pitch. The medium series, 7.6 mm, is the usual choice for general assembly.

M7×0.75 strength

ISO 898-1 tabulates fine-pitch loads only for a selected set of threads from M8×1 to M39×3, so it gives no proof or breaking loads for M7×0.75. For an estimate, multiply the tensile stress area of 31.1 mm² by the stress of the material: for example, 800 MPa gives 24.9 kN.

How the M7×0.75 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 0.75 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 0.650 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 7 − 0.487 = 6.513 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 7 − 0.812 = 6.188 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 7 − 0.920 = 6.080 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 6.296² = 31.14 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 2.10°
