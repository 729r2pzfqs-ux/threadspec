3/8-24 UNF Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

3/8-24 UNF is the Unified fine thread for the 3/8 in (9.52 mm) diameter: 24 threads per inch, a pitch of 0.04167 in (1.058 mm), on a 60° profile. ASME B1.1 lists it in the UNF series. Against the coarse 3/8-16 UNC it has 24 threads per inch instead of 16, a stress area 13% larger and a lead angle of 2.18° instead of 3.40°.

3/8-24 is used for inverted-flare brake line fittings and automotive fasteners.

3/8-24 UNF limits of size (class 2A bolt, 2B nut)

Classes 2A and 2B are the general-purpose fits for commercial bolts, screws and nuts. The 2A external thread has an allowance of 0.0011 in, so its maximum size is that much below basic; the 2B nut starts at the basic size.

3/8-24 UNF tap drill size

Tap drill for 3/8-24 UNF: Q (0.3320 in, 8.43 mm)

This leaves about 79% thread in the tapped hole.

Q is the drill listed for 3/8-24 UNF in standard tap drill charts. It is 0.0430 in under the 0.3750 in major diameter.

The 2B minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 0.330 and 0.340 in. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for 3/8-24 UNF

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest metric thread

No metric thread is interchangeable with 3/8-24 UNF. The nearest is M10×1, which is 0.48 mm larger in diameter with a pitch of 1.000 mm against 1.058 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the 3/8-24 UNF dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 0.04167 in = 1 ÷ 24 threads per inch
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 0.0361 in
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 0.3750 − 0.0271 = 0.3479 in
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 0.3750 − 0.0451 = 0.3299 in
  • d3 = d − 1.190785 × P = 0.3750 − 0.0496 = 0.3254 in (external root, 11H/16 deep)
  • As = 0.7854 × (d − 0.9743 × P)² = 0.7854 × 0.3344² = 0.08783 in²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 2.18°
