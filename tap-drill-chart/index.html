How Tap Drill Sizes Are Determined

The recommended tap drill is d − P (nominal diameter minus one pitch). This gives approximately 75% thread engagement — the industry-standard balance of joint strength against tapping torque and risk of tap breakage.

For metric threads: tap drill = d − P. For unified inch threads, refer to the published ASME B1.1 tap drill table.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size drill bit do I need for an M10 bolt?

For an M10×1.5 coarse thread, use an 8.5mm tap drill. Use d − P = 10 − 1.5 = 8.5 mm; round up to the next standard size ≥ 8.5 mm = 8.5 mm.

What is 75% thread engagement?

75% thread engagement means the thread depth is 75% of the full theoretical depth. This is the industry standard because going higher (e.g., 100%) doubles tapping torque and risk of tap breakage while only increasing strength by about 5%.

How do I calculate a tap drill size?

For metric threads: tap drill = nominal diameter − pitch (d − P). For inch threads, use the published ASME B1.1 tap drill table. Always round UP to the next standard size.
