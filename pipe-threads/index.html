Where the diameters are measured

Because the thread is tapered it has no single pitch diameter. E0 is the pitch diameter at the end of the pipe, E1 at the plane where a fitting stops when screwed on by hand, and E2 at the end of the effective thread. The three are tied together by the taper: E1 = E0 + L1 ÷ 16.

Two tap drill columns

The chart gives two drills because published sources differ. The first column is the drill ASME B1.20.1 suggests for tapping without reaming, which the standard lists up to the 2-1/2 size; above that the value is from Machinery’s Handbook. The second column is the size found on most printed shop charts. From 1/4 NPT upward those chart values match the drills the standard gives for straight pipe threads (NPSC), so they are a little large for a taper tap: easier to tap, with shallower threads at the bottom of the hole.

No drill is published for 8, 10 and 12 inch NPT. Threads of that size are bored or milled to the minor diameter K0.

Nominal size is not a dimension

NPT sizes name the pipe, following the nominal pipe size (NPS) system. A 1/2 NPT thread is cut on pipe with an outside diameter of 0.840 in (21.34 mm), and nothing on it measures half an inch. To identify a pipe thread, measure the outside diameter and count the threads per inch, then match them to the chart.

Common questions

What is the tap drill for 1/4 NPT?

The tap drill for 1/4-18 NPT is 7/16 in (0.4375 in, 11.11 mm). The standard and the usual shop charts agree on this size.

What is the tap drill for 1/2 NPT?

ASME B1.20.1 suggests 45/64 in (0.7031 in) for tapping without reaming. Many charts list 23/32 in (0.7188 in), which is easier to tap but leaves shallower threads.

Do NPT threads need sealant?

Yes. NPT threads wedge together but leave a spiral leak path at the crests and roots, so ASME B1.20.1 calls for a sealant such as PTFE tape or pipe compound for a pressure-tight joint. NPTF (Dryseal) threads are made to seal without it.
