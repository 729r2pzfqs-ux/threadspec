M12×1.75 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M12×1.75 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 12 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M12. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 1.75 mm (14.5 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M12 as a first-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M12×1.5, M12×1.25 and M12×1.

M12 is used for structural brackets, machine feet and anchor bolts.

M12×1.75 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 34 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.034 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M12×1.75 tap drill size

Tap drill for M12×1.75: 10.2 mm

This leaves about 79% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 12 − 1.75 = 10.25 mm. Tap drill tables round this to 10.2 mm, a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 10.106 and 10.441 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M12×1.75

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M12×1.75. The nearest is 1/2-13 UNC, which is 0.70 mm larger in diameter with a pitch of 1.954 mm against 1.750 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M12×1.75 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 1.75 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 1.516 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 12 − 1.137 = 10.863 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 12 − 1.894 = 10.106 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 12 − 2.147 = 9.853 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 10.358² = 84.27 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 2.94°
