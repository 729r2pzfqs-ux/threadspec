M9×1.25 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M9×1.25 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 9 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M9. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 1.25 mm (20.3 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M9 as a third-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M9×1.

M9×1.25 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 28 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.028 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M9×1.25 tap drill size

Tap drill for M9×1.25: 7.8 mm

This leaves about 74% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 9 − 1.25 = 7.75 mm. Tap drill tables round this to 7.8 mm, a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 7.647 and 7.912 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Clearance hole for M9

ISO 273 does not list a clearance hole for M9; its table goes from M8 to M10. The neighbouring sizes leave 0.4 to 0.5 mm of clearance in the fine series and about 1 mm in the medium series.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M9×1.25. The nearest is 3/8-24 UNF, which is 0.52 mm larger in diameter with a pitch of 1.058 mm against 1.250 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M9×1.25 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 1.25 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 1.083 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 9 − 0.812 = 8.188 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 9 − 1.353 = 7.647 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 9 − 1.534 = 7.466 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 7.827² = 48.12 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 2.78°
