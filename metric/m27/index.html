M27×3 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M27×3 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 27 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M27. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 3 mm (8.5 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M27 as a second-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M27×2, M27×1.5 and M27×1.

M27×3 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 48 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.048 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M27×3 tap drill size

Tap drill for M27×3: 24 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 27 − 3 = 24 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 23.752 and 24.252 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M27×3

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

Clearance hole for M27

The clearance hole depends only on the 27 mm diameter, not on the pitch. The medium series, 30 mm, is the usual choice for general assembly.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M27×3. The nearest is 1-8 UNC, which is 1.60 mm smaller in diameter with a pitch of 3.175 mm against 3.000 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M27×3 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 3 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 2.598 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 27 − 1.949 = 25.051 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 27 − 3.248 = 23.752 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 27 − 3.681 = 23.319 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 24.185² = 459.41 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 2.18°
