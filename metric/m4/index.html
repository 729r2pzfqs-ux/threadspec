M4×0.7 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M4×0.7 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 4 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M4. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 0.7 mm (36.3 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M4 as a first-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M4×0.5.

M4 is widely used for equipment panels, VESA 75 and VESA 100 monitor mounts and DIN rail hardware.

M4×0.7 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 22 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.022 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M4×0.7 tap drill size

Tap drill for M4×0.7: 3.3 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 4 − 0.7 = 3.3 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 3.242 and 3.422 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M4×0.7

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

Clearance hole for M4

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M4×0.7. The nearest is #8-36 UNF, which is 0.17 mm larger in diameter with a pitch of 0.706 mm against 0.700 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M4×0.7 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 0.7 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 0.606 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 4 − 0.455 = 3.545 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 4 − 0.758 = 3.242 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 4 − 0.859 = 3.141 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 3.343² = 8.78 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 3.60°
