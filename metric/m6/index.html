M6×1 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M6×1 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 6 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M6. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 1 mm (25.4 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M6 as a first-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M6×0.75.

M6 is the standard cage-nut thread for 19 inch racks in Europe and is used throughout automotive trim, machine guards and furniture fittings.

M6×1 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 26 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.026 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M6×1 tap drill size

Tap drill for M6×1: 5 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 6 − 1 = 5 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 4.917 and 5.153 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M6×1

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

Clearance hole for M6

The clearance hole depends only on the 6 mm diameter, not on the pitch. The medium series, 6.6 mm, is the usual choice for general assembly.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M6×1. The nearest is 1/4-28 UNF, which is 0.35 mm larger in diameter with a pitch of 0.907 mm against 1.000 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M6×1 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 1 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 0.866 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 6 − 0.650 = 5.350 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 6 − 1.083 = 4.917 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 6 − 1.227 = 4.773 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 5.062² = 20.12 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 3.40°
