M10×1.5 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M10×1.5 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 10 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M10. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 1.5 mm (16.9 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M10 as a first-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M10×1.25, M10×1 and M10×0.75.

M10 is used for machine frames, vehicle suspension brackets and light structural work.

M10×1.5 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 32 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.032 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M10×1.5 tap drill size

Tap drill for M10×1.5: 8.5 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 10 − 1.5 = 8.5 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 8.376 and 8.676 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M10×1.5

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M10×1.5. The nearest is 3/8-16 UNC, which is 0.48 mm smaller in diameter with a pitch of 1.587 mm against 1.500 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M10×1.5 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 1.5 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 1.299 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 10 − 0.974 = 9.026 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 10 − 1.624 = 8.376 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 10 − 1.840 = 8.160 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 8.593² = 57.99 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 3.03°
