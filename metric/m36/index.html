M36×4 Thread Dimensions and Tap Drill

M36×4 is the ISO metric coarse thread for 36 mm fasteners, which is why it is usually written simply as M36. It has a 60° profile with a pitch of 4 mm (6.3 threads per inch). ISO 261 lists M36 as a first-choice diameter. Fine-pitch versions of the same diameter are M36×3, M36×2 and M36×1.5.

M36×4 tolerance limits (6g bolt, 6H nut)

6g and 6H are the general-purpose tolerance classes for commercial screws and nuts. The 6g screw has a fundamental deviation of 60 µm, so even at its largest it sits 0.060 mm below the basic size and leaves room for a thin plating.

M36×4 tap drill size

Tap drill for M36×4: 32 mm

This leaves about 77% thread in the tapped hole.

The rule for metric tap drills is diameter minus pitch: 36 − 4 = 32 mm, which is a stocked drill size.

The 6H minor diameter of the finished nut thread must lie between 31.670 and 32.270 mm. The recommended drill falls inside that band.

Other drills that work for M36×4

A larger drill cuts less thread and taps more easily; a smaller one gives a fuller thread but loads the tap harder.

Clearance hole for M36

The clearance hole depends only on the 36 mm diameter, not on the pitch. The medium series, 39 mm, is the usual choice for general assembly.

When to choose a coarse or a fine thread

Closest inch thread

No inch thread is interchangeable with M36×4. The nearest is 1-3/8-6 UNC, which is 1.08 mm smaller in diameter with a pitch of 4.233 mm against 4.000 mm. A nut may start on the wrong thread and then bind or strip, so identify by both diameter and pitch.

How the M36×4 dimensions are calculated

Every basic dimension follows from the major diameter and the pitch:

  • P = 4 mm
  • H = 0.866025 × P = 3.464 mm
  • d2 = d − 0.649519 × P = 36 − 2.598 = 33.402 mm
  • D1 = d − 1.082532 × P = 36 − 4.330 = 31.670 mm
  • d3 = d − 1.226869 × P = 36 − 4.907 = 31.093 mm (external root, 17H/24 deep)
  • As = π/4 × ((d2 + d3) ÷ 2)² = π/4 × 32.247² = 816.72 mm²
  • Lead angle = arctan(P ÷ (π × d2)) = 2.18°
